\begin{abstract} 
Citrus fruit diseases cause severe yield reductions and financial losses in global agriculture. Rapid identification of these diseases is necessary to prevent widespread crop damage. In this work, four pre-trained deep convolutional neural networks (MobileNetV2, InceptionV3, ResNet50, and VGG19) are evaluated for automated citrus disease recognition. The experimental dataset contains 1,463 high-resolution images divided into four classes: canker, black spot, greening, and healthy fruit. Each architecture is fine-tuned using transfer learning and compared across classification metrics (accuracy, precision, recall, F1-score) as well as computational requirements (training duration, inference time, memory footprint). MobileNetV2 achieved the best overall performance, reaching 96.77\% classification accuracy, 98.54\% F1-score, a training time of 406.29~s, and a memory size of only 9.82~MB. InceptionV3 reached 96.59\% test accuracy with higher memory requirements (22.81~MB). ResNet50 showed lower classification performance with an accuracy of 58.21\%, while VGG19 required excessive training time (3360.14~s) and memory (118.59~MB). These empirical results demonstrate that lightweight models like MobileNetV2 provide an effective balance between accuracy and computational cost, making them well-suited for practical deployment on portable and edge agricultural devices.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Agriculture forms a core pillar of the economy in Bangladesh, supporting rural employment and national food security. Modern agricultural management focuses on raising crop yield and quality while controlling production costs \cite{p1,p2,p19}. With population growth and climate shifts, precision agriculture techniques are increasingly used to monitor field conditions \cite{p10,p21}. These methods apply computational vision for pest tracking, weed management, yield prediction, and automated crop disease identification \cite{p3}.

Citrus production in Bangladesh has expanded steadily over the past few decades. Total annual output increased from 19,163 tonnes in 1973 to 175,325 tonnes in 2022, reflecting an average growth rate of 5.61\% per year \cite{p22}. Field surveys in the citrus-growing regions of Sylhet indicate that canker, scab, dieback, and greening (Huanglongbing) represent major threats to commercial orchards \cite{p5,p19}. Detecting these infections early is vital to prevent orchard-wide spread. Convolutional neural networks (CNNs) have become a standard approach for image-based plant disease recognition \cite{p5}. Architectures such as AlexNet, VGG16, VGG19, InceptionV3, ResNet, and DenseNet have shown strong feature extraction capabilities on botanical image datasets.

\subsection{Context and Background}

Plant diseases in agrarian economies place direct pressure on smallholder farmers and domestic food supplies \cite{p6,p8}. Crop cultivation provides both food and primary household income across rural Bangladesh. Sudden pathogen outbreaks often lower harvest yields, making timely plant protection a central concern \cite{p6}. Digital imaging combined with deep learning offers a non-invasive way to detect disease symptoms from visual patterns \cite{p19,p20}. Automated lesion screening allows targeted chemical treatments, reducing pesticide overuse. Official surveys show that fruit and crop farming spans over 39,678 acres across the country, indicating a clear need for practical, automated diagnostic tools \cite{p7,p6}.

\subsection{Research Motivation}

Previous research has examined deep neural networks for identifying citrus leaf and fruit diseases \cite{p2, p5, p8}. Although deep networks such as AlexNet and VGG19 achieve high recognition rates, they tend to carry large parameter sets, high memory demands, and long training times \cite{p5}. Most existing studies focus primarily on accuracy metrics without measuring inference speed, memory consumption, or edge hardware compatibility. To address this gap, this study conducts an empirical comparison evaluating both diagnostic accuracy and computational resource demands across four popular transfer learning models.

\subsection{Objectives and Scope}
The primary objectives of this study are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item To fine-tune four pre-trained CNN architectures (MobileNetV2, InceptionV3, ResNet50, and VGG19) on a four-class citrus fruit disease dataset.
    \item To evaluate both classification accuracy (precision, recall, F1-score, overall accuracy) and resource metrics (training duration, test inference latency, parameter counts, memory usage).
    \item To identify lightweight, efficient models suitable for practical deployment on resource-limited agricultural devices.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Significance of Research}
This work provides quantitative benchmark data on the trade-offs between classification accuracy and hardware requirements for citrus disease identification. The experimental results give practical guidelines for engineers and agronomists developing computer vision tools for smart farming applications.

\subsection{Dataset Overview and Preparation}
The experimental dataset contains 1,463 RGB images collected from PlantVillage and Kaggle repositories. Four distinct condition classes are included:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Canker}: Raised corky lesions caused by the bacterium \textit{Xanthomonas citri}.
    \item \textbf{Black Spot}: Necrotic fungal spots caused by \textit{Phyllosticta citricarpa}.
    \item \textbf{Greening (HLB)}: Mottled yellowing caused by vector-spread bacterial infection.
    \item \textbf{Healthy}: Asymptomatic fruit samples free from visual defects.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Data Pre-Processing and Augmentation}
To prevent overfitting and improve generalization against real-world variations, data augmentation was applied during training using dynamic image generators:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Pixel rescaling to the range $[0, 1]$.
    \item Random rotation within $[-20^\circ, +20^\circ]$.
    \item Horizontal and vertical shifts up to 20\%.
    \item Random shear transformations.
    \item Random zoom adjustments up to 20\%.
    \item Random horizontal flipping.
\end{itemize}
All input images were standardized to $224 \times 224 \times 3$ pixels and processed in mini-batches of 32 images.

\subsection{Optimization and Training Configuration}
All models were trained using the Adam optimizer with a learning rate of $\eta = 0.0001$. Categorical cross-entropy was used as the loss function:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{CCE} = -\sum_{i=1}^{N} \sum_{c=1}^{C} y_{i,c} \log(\hat{y}_{i,c})
\end{equation}
where $N$ is the batch size, $C=4$ is the number of target classes, $y_{i,c}$ represents the ground-truth binary indicator, and $\hat{y}_{i,c}$ is the predicted probability for class $c$. Training was carried out for 50 epochs with model checkpointing based on validation loss.

\subsection{Evaluation Metrics}

\subsubsection{Diagnostic Performance Metrics}
Model classification performance was evaluated using standard statistical indicators:
\begin{equation}
    \text{Accuracy} = \frac{TP + TN}{TP + TN + FP + FN}
    \label{eq:accuracy}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    \text{Precision} = \frac{TP}{TP + FP}
    \label{eq:precision}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    \text{Recall} = \frac{TP}{TP + FN}
    \label{eq:recall}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    F1\text{-Score} = \frac{2 \cdot \text{Precision} \cdot \text{Recall}}{\text{Precision} + \text{Recall}}
    \label{eq:f1score}
\end{equation}
where $TP$, $TN$, $FP$, and $FN$ represent True Positives, True Negatives, False Positives, and False Negatives, respectively.

\subsubsection{Computational Complexity Metrics}
Computational efficiency was evaluated through four parameters: total training time (seconds), test inference time (seconds), total parameter count, and memory allocation (MB).

\subsection{Comparative Discussion and Deployment Feasibility}

The experimental data shows that MobileNetV2 delivers the most effective balance of high accuracy and low resource usage. The inverted residual structure extracts rich visual features with few parameters. InceptionV3 reached similar accuracy, but required more memory (22.81~MB) and longer training (717.00~s). ResNet50 struggled under fixed-feature extraction, and VGG19 had high computational and memory costs.

These findings indicate that MobileNetV2 is the most practical option for deployment in field conditions, such as smartphone applications, portable scanning tools, and drone-mounted cameras for real-time orchard monitoring.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This study presented an empirical evaluation of four transfer learning architectures for citrus fruit disease diagnosis. Evaluating MobileNetV2, InceptionV3, ResNet50, and VGG19 on a four-class dataset showed that MobileNetV2 provided the best overall performance. It achieved 96.77\% accuracy, 98.54\% F1-score, a training time of 406.29~s, and a memory footprint of 9.82~MB. These results demonstrate that lightweight convolutional networks can achieve high diagnostic accuracy while maintaining low computational demands.

\subsection{Limitations}
The primary limitation of this work is the dataset size (1,463 images) and single-source acquisition environment. Field orchards often present variable lighting, partial occlusions, and overlapping leaf symptoms that require testing on larger multi-source datasets.

\subsection{Future Scope}
Future work will explore several avenues:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Testing metaheuristic optimization algorithms (such as Grey Wolf Optimizer or Particle Swarm Optimization) for automated hyperparameter tuning.
    \item Developing lightweight hybrid vision transformer models for edge devices.
    \item Incorporating visual explanation techniques, such as Grad-CAM, to highlight lesion regions and assist agricultural field workers.
\end{itemize}
