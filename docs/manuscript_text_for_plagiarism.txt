ABSTRACT

Keywords

Citrus fruit diseases cause severe yield reductions and financial losses in global agriculture. Rapid identification of these
diseases is necessary to prevent widespread crop damage. In
this work, four pre-trained deep convolutional neural networks
(MobileNetV2, InceptionV3, ResNet50, and VGG19) are evaluated
for automated citrus disease recognition. The experimental
dataset contains 1,463 high-resolution images divided into four
classes: canker, black spot, greening, and healthy fruit. Each
architecture is fine-tuned using transfer learning and compared
across classification metrics (accuracy, precision, recall, F1-score)
as well as computational requirements (training duration, inference
time, memory footprint). MobileNetV2 achieved the best overall
performance, reaching 96.77% classification accuracy, 98.54%
F1-score, a training time of 406.29 s, and a memory size of
only 9.82 MB. InceptionV3 reached 96.59% test accuracy with
higher memory requirements (22.81 MB). ResNet50 showed lower
classification performance with an accuracy of 58.21%, while
VGG19 required excessive training time (3360.14 s) and memory
(118.59 MB). These empirical results demonstrate that lightweight
models like MobileNetV2 provide an effective balance between
accuracy and computational cost, making them well-suited for
practical deployment on portable and edge agricultural devices.

1.

INTRODUCTION

Agriculture forms a core pillar of the economy in Bangladesh,
supporting rural employment and national food security. Modern
agricultural management focuses on raising crop yield and quality
while controlling production costs [1, 2, 3]. With population growth
and climate shifts, precision agriculture techniques are increasingly
used to monitor field conditions [4, 5]. These methods apply
computational vision for pest tracking, weed management, yield
prediction, and automated crop disease identification [6].
Citrus production in Bangladesh has expanded steadily over the
past few decades. Total annual output increased from 19,163 tonnes
in 1973 to 175,325 tonnes in 2022, reflecting an average growth
rate of 5.61% per year [7]. Field surveys in the citrus-growing
regions of Sylhet indicate that canker, scab, dieback, and greening
(Huanglongbing) represent major threats to commercial orchards
[8, 3]. Detecting these infections early is vital to prevent
orchard-wide spread. Convolutional neural networks (CNNs)
have become a standard approach for image-based plant disease
recognition [8]. Architectures such as AlexNet, VGG16, VGG19,
InceptionV3, ResNet, and DenseNet have shown strong feature
extraction capabilities on botanical image datasets.

1.1

Context and Background

Plant diseases in agrarian economies place direct pressure on
smallholder farmers and domestic food supplies [9, 10]. Crop
cultivation provides both food and primary household income
across rural Bangladesh. Sudden pathogen outbreaks often lower
harvest yields, making timely plant protection a central concern [9].
Digital imaging combined with deep learning offers a non-invasive
way to detect disease symptoms from visual patterns [3, 11].
Automated lesion screening allows targeted chemical treatments,
reducing pesticide overuse. Official surveys show that fruit and
crop farming spans over 39,678 acres across the country, indicating
a clear need for practical, automated diagnostic tools [12, 9].

1.2

Research Motivation

Previous research has examined deep neural networks for
identifying citrus leaf and fruit diseases [2, 8, 10]. Although deep
networks such as AlexNet and VGG19 achieve high recognition
rates, they tend to carry large parameter sets, high memory
demands, and long training times [8]. Most existing studies focus
primarily on accuracy metrics without measuring inference speed,
memory consumption, or edge hardware compatibility. To address
this gap, this study conducts an empirical comparison evaluating
both diagnostic accuracy and computational resource demands
across four popular transfer learning models.

1.3

Objectives and Scope

The primary objectives of this study are:
• To fine-tune four pre-trained CNN architectures (MobileNetV2, InceptionV3, ResNet50, and VGG19) on a
four-class citrus fruit disease dataset.
• To evaluate both classification accuracy (precision, recall,
F1-score, overall accuracy) and resource metrics (training
duration, test inference latency, parameter counts, memory
usage).
• To identify lightweight, efficient models suitable for practical
deployment on resource-limited agricultural devices.

1.4

Significance of Research

This work provides quantitative benchmark data on the trade-offs
between classification accuracy and hardware requirements for
citrus disease identification. The experimental results give practical
guidelines for engineers and agronomists developing computer
vision tools for smart farming applications.
The rest of this paper is structured as follows. Section 2 reviews
relevant literature on plant and citrus disease classification.
Section 3 details the dataset, pre-processing steps, and transfer
learning methodology. Section 4 presents the experimental results,
performance comparisons, and computational analyses. Section 5
concludes the paper and highlights directions for future research.

LITERATURE REVIEW

Agriculture supports approximately 87% of rural households in
Bangladesh through direct farming and related supply chains [13].
Automated visual diagnosis of crop diseases has therefore received
considerable attention from researchers.
Ahmed and Muqit [2] applied deep neural networks to recognize
citrus fruit and leaf infections. Their approach involved image
acquisition, pre-processing, and CNN classification. Testing on
2,293 images from the PlantVillage dataset achieved 94.55%

Balafas et al. [6] compared traditional machine learning models
(Support Vector Machines, Random Forests, Stochastic Gradient
Descent) with deep models (InceptionV3, VGG16, VGG19)
on citrus leaf images. Their experiments showed that deep
convolutional architectures consistently outperformed traditional
classifiers under variable field lighting and complex backgrounds.
Elaraby et al. [8] examined deep learning methods for detecting
black spot, canker, greening, and melanose using AlexNet and
VGG19, obtaining 94% accuracy on a multi-class dataset. Sujatha
et al. [4] proposed a mobile-oriented plant leaf disease recognition
pipeline using deep CNNs for on-site diagnosis. Hasan et
al. [9, 22] utilized transfer learning to classify regional crop
diseases in Bangladesh, finding that pre-trained ImageNet weights
substantially speed up network convergence when working with
limited training images. Table 1 provides a structured summary of
related literature on citrus and crop disease detection.

PROPOSED METHODOLOGY

The experimental methodology consists of six main steps:
dataset collection, image partitioning, data augmentation, model
construction via transfer learning, training with hyperparameter
tuning, and quantitative metric evaluation. Figure 1 outlines the
overall workflow.

3.1

Dataset Overview and Preparation

The experimental dataset contains 1,463 RGB images collected
from PlantVillage and Kaggle repositories. Four distinct condition
classes are included:
• Canker: Raised corky lesions caused by the bacterium
Xanthomonas citri.
• Black Spot: Necrotic fungal spots caused by Phyllosticta
citricarpa.
• Greening (HLB): Mottled yellowing caused by vector-spread
bacterial infection.
• Healthy: Asymptomatic fruit samples free from visual defects.

4.3

Comparative Discussion and Deployment
Feasibility

The experimental data shows that MobileNetV2 delivers the most
effective balance of high accuracy and low resource usage. The
inverted residual structure extracts rich visual features with few
parameters. InceptionV3 reached similar accuracy, but required
more memory (22.81 MB) and longer training (717.00 s).
ResNet50 struggled under fixed-feature extraction, and VGG19 had
high computational and memory costs.
These findings indicate that MobileNetV2 is the most practical
option for deployment in field conditions, such as smartphone
applications, portable scanning tools, and drone-mounted cameras
for real-time orchard monitoring.

5.

CONCLUSION

This study presented an empirical evaluation of four transfer
learning architectures for citrus fruit disease diagnosis. Evaluating
MobileNetV2, InceptionV3, ResNet50, and VGG19 on a four-class
dataset showed that MobileNetV2 provided the best overall
performance. It achieved 96.77% accuracy, 98.54% F1-score, a
training time of 406.29 s, and a memory footprint of 9.82 MB.
These results demonstrate that lightweight convolutional networks
can achieve high diagnostic accuracy while maintaining low
computational demands.

5.1

Limitations

The primary limitation of this work is the dataset size (1,463
images) and single-source acquisition environment. Field orchards
often present variable lighting, partial occlusions, and overlapping
leaf symptoms that require testing on larger multi-source datasets.

5.2

Future Scope

Future work will explore several avenues:
• Testing metaheuristic optimization algorithms (such as Grey
Wolf Optimizer or Particle Swarm Optimization) for automated hyperparameter tuning.
